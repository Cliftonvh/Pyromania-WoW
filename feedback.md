# Thorium WoW Feedback
These are going to be based on the little time that I have played on Thorium WoW.

## Starting Out on Thorium-WoW
Starting out on Thorium-WoW is quite an interesting experience. There is a small quest-line designed to help new players familiarize themselves with the Mall and Thorium-WoW, which is fantastic!

However, a problem arises when new characters are created by players who have already completed the introductory quest-line multiple times. Repeating the same quest-line can lead to a repetitive and tedious gameplay loop.

**Suggestion:** Introduce an option for players to choose whether they want to redo the introductory quest-line. This would enhance the gameplay experience for returning players.

### Starting Quests Feedback
**Pros:**
- Provides an excellent introduction for newcomers to start playing Thorium-WoW.

**Cons:**
- Becomes tedious when repeated for every new character.
- The quests are very linear, lacking variety.
- (Subjective) Completing the quest-line felt more like a chore than an engaging storyline. Which I wanted to follow if there is more thought in both Map Design and Quest Design.

## Upgrading Armor and Current Content / Progression

I've played Thorium-WoW for a few hours now, and the current content feels lacking in terms of fun and engagement. Specifically, the Armor and Dungeons system feels more like a grind-fest rather than an engaging way to earn upgraded gear. Now the problem with this, is that players who just start out, like myself will not get that hook for Thorium with the current system, and there is a chance these players might leave sooner because of the frustrations in the system.

### Questline Issues
I had to run Deadmines three times, and I encountered an annoying quest that had to be accepted individually each time. I only noticed this quest when I randomly returned to the Mall, forcing me to run Deadmines a fourth time to complete the quest.

This system's flow is frustrating and could be improved by providing clearer objectives for the player, and not have any repeating quests that require the exact same thing to be done.

### Upgrade Armor Feedback
Having experience managing a WoW fun/custom server, I've dealt with armor upgrading systems before and some of the tweaking/ideas needed to create a well designed and throughout system. We've gathered significant feedback and understand the importance of providing new armor at appropriate times, as such the current system increases and progresses the armor too fast, and currently it skips almost 3-4 upgrades without players having to consider why they are upgrading.

The current system has a few notable flaws:

1. **Currency Overload:**
   - Upgrading gear involves obtaining various amounts of random currency, creating a repetitive gameplay loop: get 4-5 different tokens, upgrade using 1-3 different tokens, repeat.
   - For newcomers, especially those unfamiliar with Fun Servers, the sheer amount of currency from dungeons and other activities can be overwhelming, and this is exactly what many Fun Servers in my opinion do wrong. It seems that adding a lot of random currency results in good Game Design, which (imo) it does not.
   - I'd like to think of more ways to help Thorium-WoW beat this issue, and create a story Driven and well thoughtout server that utilizes good Game practices in order to create an amazing experience.
   - The current currency is of Epic rarity, but players obtain it merely by killing a few mobs. This diminishes the sense of achievement and excitement, as rarities are typically associated with the perceived value and rarity of an item. When rare items are too easily obtained, it reduces their appeal and the overall dopamine response for players.

2. **Reward Choices:**
    - New players may struggle to choose the best reward, leading to random selections, because of the sheer amount of random emblems and currency the player can obtain. This is a game design issue because it removes the thoughtful decision-making aspect that many players love about WoW.

3. **Repeating Quests and content:**
    - I've played for a few hours, and all I had done was run Deadmines 3 times (with my 4th forced), go to a random island/dungeon for pirate stuff and kill pirates?

4. **Upgrade Gap:**
    - The player is going through Deadmines maybe 3-5 times, without needing any of the upgrades. But when it's time to upgrade, I've just upgraded 4-5 tiers instantly without having to check what the upgrades do, and what the changes are.
    - Currently the first Deadmines Dungeon is way too easy. The player should start off at a lower geared, less health and damage and having to think how to run deadmines, certain rooms and not go in blindly. I've ran Deadmines without having to heal myself once, and ran through 3 rooms without taking too much damage, and being able to almost instantly kill full rooms without much difficulty.
    - Having to run older Dungeons is an amazing thing to do, but it also creates this frustration when you have to do it for shits and giggles for 3 - 5 times, just to upgrade a specific piece multiple times.

5. **Leveling doesn't do anything:**
    - I ran deadmines, and I was level 85 after. What is the purpose of Leveling, if there is no sense of progression because of it.
    - Currently the Blood Leveling uses the regular Level Up FX, it might be interesting to use a Red one, considering it's name. Better yet, do purple, that shit is cool!
    - Leveling is be one of the biggest traits of World of Warcraft, and from 80 - 85 seems to be just a random thought to have players level a small amount of levels just for the sake of it.

  - Consider reducing the number of different currencies needed for upgrades and game. This would make the system more accessible and less overwhelming for newcomers.
  - Too many currencies will becomes easier overtime, but like Retail, make sure to help ease the player into the currency, and don't just give them everything at once. It's essential for old and new players to understand the significance of these currencies and their place in the World.

## Overall Game Design
Much of the game design focuses on high stats and massive damage, but this approach is challenging to balance.

### Balancing:
  - High stats often lead to significant balancing issues, especially when they do not align with the original base game mechanics of WOTLK 3.3.5a classes.

### Dopamine:
  - High damage numbers provide an initial dopamine rush, but this can lead to diminished interest if there's insufficient progression (e.g., from 139k damage to 145k damage).
  - Excessive dopamine spikes can cause players to experience a dopamine crash, leading to exhaustion and loss of interest.
  - Rapid progression with minimal effort reduces the sense of accomplishment and long-term engagement.

### Suggestions for Improvement

#### Reduce Stats:
  - Instead of continuously increasing stats, consider reducing them slightly to facilitate better balance. Align content with these adjusted stats.
  - Lower stats/damage/health will help players more easily understand the impact of their gear and how much stronger they become from Level 80 to 85 through new gear acquisition.
  - Get rid of random .buff and have players actually find others to buff themselves. Create a sense of community and don't hand out too much to players, but let them work for it. Currently, content should be based off when players have .buff on, rather than not having it. Instead you should consider that content should be made for non-.buff so players won't have it too easy.
  - I can see where 255 and Fun Servers get their appeal, but I also think that most players will have a higher appreciation to content that is balanced, and thoughout. Ensuring that they can enjoy content with not only themselves, but also others. 
  - I can see a problem currently, where High Ranking players might get stuck doing nothing but grind (alone) and these new stats will ensure that the end game to achieve this is in a later stage, helping with players feeling of progression and combatting these issues where people might have nothing to do.
  - Content seems to be a little too easy (Correct me if I am wrong, this is just from what I can see as a new player)
  - I have checked and Dungeon Mobs don't do the correct Spell Damage, and using lower stats, will fix this issue, otherwise you'd have to do a lot of work reworking all spells, to make sure the content/dungeons are actually using correct spell damages
  - Spell costs of Classes also do not scale with the current High Stat system that is in place. This makes it so current classes that use mana will always have an advantage over Rogue and such.
